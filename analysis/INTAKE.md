# Data intake checklist

Fill these into `inputs.py`. Items marked **⏰** are time-sensitive — they expire
or lose value with delay. Items marked **🔒** are irreversible once done.

## ⏰ Do these before gathering anything else

- [ ] **⏰ File for Social Security survivor benefits.** Retroactivity is
      generally capped at ~6 months. Bring: death certificate, marriage
      certificate, child's birth certificate, both SSNs, his W-2 or
      self-employment tax return for the most recent year.
- [ ] **⏰ Request the $255 lump-sum death payment** (must generally be applied
      for within 2 years).
- [ ] **⏰ Georgia year's support petition** — time-limited; ask the probate
      attorney.
- [ ] **🔒 Do NOT sign any 401(k) distribution or rollover form** until §2 and
      §3.6 of PRESSURE_TEST.md are resolved. A rollover into her own IRA cannot
      be undone and costs the penalty exception until 59½. A rollover also
      destroys NUA treatment on employer stock.

## People

- [ ] Her exact date of birth
- [ ] Child's exact date of birth (drives SS cutoffs at 16/18/19 and the QSS and
      HOH filing windows)
- [ ] Is the child a full-time student? (extends dependency to 23)
- [ ] Any other dependents
- [ ] Date of death

## Income

- [ ] Her gross annual salary, and whether it is stable
- [ ] Her employer benefits: health coverage, 401(k) match, HSA, life insurance
- [ ] **His W-2 / 1099 income earned in 2026 before death** (drives the 2026 MFJ
      return)
- [ ] His final-year withholding already paid
- [ ] **His Social Security PIA** — from his most recent SSA statement, or ask
      SSA at the survivor appointment
- [ ] Any pension, deferred comp, or unpaid bonus owed to his estate

## The house

- [ ] Remaining mortgage principal (current payoff quote, not the statement)
- [ ] **Exact interest rate**
- [ ] Monthly payment, split P&I vs escrow
- [ ] Months remaining
- [ ] Is it escrowed?
- [ ] Whose name is on the note and on the deed?
- [ ] Annual property tax (Cobb County bill) and current homestead exemptions
- [ ] Homeowners insurance annual premium
- [ ] Current home value
- [ ] **Any mortgage life / credit life insurance on the loan?** (may pay it off
      outright)
- [ ] Does the servicer permit a **recast**, and what is the fee and minimum
      principal reduction?
- [ ] HOA dues

## Retirement and other assets

- [ ] Total inherited 401(k) balance
- [ ] **Traditional vs Roth split** (and the Roth's 5-year clock start)
- [ ] **Does the 401(k) hold employer stock?** If yes — cost basis and current
      value, for the NUA analysis. Ask before moving anything.
- [ ] **Does the plan permit partial / periodic distributions to a beneficiary,
      or does it force a lump sum?** Get this in writing from the plan
      administrator.
- [ ] Was she named sole primary beneficiary?
- [ ] His age at death and whether he had reached his required beginning date
      (determines the SECURE 2.0 §327 election)
- [ ] Taxable brokerage / joint accounts — **value at date of death** (the new
      basis) and current value
- [ ] Cash and emergency savings
- [ ] **Life insurance proceeds** — individual and employer group
- [ ] His IRAs, HSA, annuities
- [ ] Her own retirement accounts
- [ ] 529 / education savings for the child

## Liabilities

- [ ] Any other debt, with rates — car loans, credit cards, student loans,
      medical bills from his final illness
- [ ] Funeral and estate-administration costs still outstanding

## Spending

- [ ] Actual monthly spending, from 6–12 months of bank and card statements —
      not an estimate
- [ ] Which expenses were his alone and now stop
- [ ] New costs: childcare, health insurance if she was on his plan, counseling
- [ ] The honest number: what is the monthly deficit **after** SS survivor
      benefits are in payment?

## Filing

- [ ] Confirm she is not remarrying in 2026 (would end MFJ)
- [ ] Who prepared their prior returns; get 2024 and 2025 copies
- [ ] Whether itemizing beats the standard deduction (mortgage interest at 2–3%
      is small, but the SALT cap is $40,400 for 2026 — property tax plus state
      income tax may push her over $32,200)

## Professional review

- [ ] CPA — 2026 final joint return and the multi-year withdrawal plan
- [ ] Fee-only fiduciary (hourly / flat-fee, **not** commission) — account
      titling and the NUA question
- [ ] Estate attorney — probate, year's support, deed transfer, and updating
      **her own** will and beneficiary designations
