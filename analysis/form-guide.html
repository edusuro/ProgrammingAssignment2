Which boxes to tick, page by page

The whole packet comes down to one decision asked three times, three sections to fill in, and the rest left completely empty. One thing needs sorting out before anything is signed.

The one thing to sort out first

Page 3 has been filled in, and it probably shouldn't be.

Section 3 is headed "See-Through Trust, Trust Information" and is only for someone acting as trustee of a trust. Her name, address, Social Security number and date of birth are written into the Trustee block, with a successor trustee named underneath.

If she inherits this personally — which the "Spouse Acceptance" title on every page suggests — Section 3 should be entirely blank. This matters more than it looks: the signature page has her certifying "If I am a spouse beneficiary of a See-Through Trust, I have consulted with the Trustee… and TIAA has relied upon the information I have provided in calculating my RMDs." Signing with that section filled in makes a statement about how the money is held.

What to do: call TIAA at 800-842-2252, confirm whether the named beneficiary is her or a trust, and ask for a clean form if it's her. Don't cross it out — the form says it is void if altered.

Resolved — he was 50

That clears most of the frightening parts of this packet.

Required withdrawals don't begin until age 75, so he was twenty-five years away from ever having to take one. Nothing was owed for 2026, which empties two sections outright.

It also defuses Section 8. The form says that when someone dies before reaching withdrawal age, the surviving spouse must use the table that produces the smaller required amounts — so the favourable treatment is automatic rather than something she has to elect. The irrevocable box is probably moot. Still worth having TIAA confirm that on the phone, but it is no longer a decision hanging over the form.

And her own required withdrawals won't start until roughly 2051, the year he would have turned 75 — about two years later than if the money were in her own name. A small bonus on top of the penalty exemption.

Still open

Page 1 of the packet — the one listing the actual accounts and balances — hasn't been reviewed. It settles the question below, and should also state the deadline. Page 1 notes a 60-day settlement period for at least one account type, after which the choices narrow to the two we don't want.

The one decision, asked three times

Three different kinds of account came with the inheritance, and each has its own page asking whether she wants it in her name or kept in his. All three get the same answer: keep it in his.

Are these accounts real, or just boilerplate?

A fair thing to be suspicious about — this is clearly a master template, and it does include pages that plainly don't apply, like the section for deaths before 2021. But the IRA and ATRA pages look genuinely inserted, not generic.

Two reasons. Both open with a flat statement of fact rather than a condition: "Your inheritance includes an IRA." "Your inheritance includes an ATRA." Every other optional passage in the packet is phrased as "If you are inheriting…". And where ordinary pages are numbered "Page 5 of 17," these two are labelled "Page S_IRA" and the equivalent for the ATRA — a separate code, of the kind used for supplements that get added only when they apply.

So: probably real. But don't act on the ATRA idea until it's confirmed, because the tax angle only exists if the account does. Page 1 lists the accounts and balances, and TIAA can confirm in one call.

4Section

The retirement plan

Tick Option 1

"Stay in the plan as a beneficiary." Leave Option 1A (partial cash withdrawal) empty — she can request money any time later without committing to an amount now.

Check the "All Accounts" box above it so the choice covers every plan account, and ask TIAA to confirm that's the right way to do it when several accounts are involved.

5Section 4

Roll over to my own account · Full cash withdrawal

Leave both empty

Option 2 is the one that costs about $30,000 in penalties. Option 3 is the one that costs about $92,000 over ten years. Neither box gets touched.

IRAMandatory

Inheriting an IRA

Tick Option 2

"Remain as a spouse beneficiary under my spouse's original contract." Not Option 1.

TIAA's own wording under Option 1: "10% tax penalty for withdrawals under age 59½… Inheritance of the account is not an exception with respect to your own IRA." Under Option 2: "No tax penalties for taking withdrawals prior to age 59½."

ATRAMandatory

Inheriting an After-Tax Retirement Annuity

Tick Option 2

"I elect to be treated as a designated beneficiary under my spouse's original contract." Again, not Option 1 — that one says plainly, "Death of your spouse is not an exception."

Worth asking about separately: an after-tax annuity holds money that was already taxed once, so part of it may come back out tax-free. If a decent share of the balance is here, it could be the cheapest place to draw the mortgage money from.

6Section 5

Tax withholding on regular payments

Ask first

This sets withholding on a recurring payment stream — and the form calls those streams "irrevocable." She isn't setting one up, so there may be nothing here to withhold from. Ask TIAA whether it must be completed at all when no payments are being scheduled.

Leave blank

This would only apply if a withdrawal he was required to take in 2026 were still owed. At 50 he was twenty-five years short of that. Nothing owed, nothing to complete.

Leave blank

10Section 8

Required withdrawals — deaths from 2020 on

Confirm, then it's easy

Do not tick "10-Year Rule." It's optional, it's only for IRAs, and it would force the whole account out within ten years for no reason.

The irrevocable election below it was the one worrying part of this packet, and his age has largely defused it: because he died decades before reaching withdrawal age, the form says she must use the favourable table regardless. Ask TIAA to confirm that reading, then follow whatever they say. Either way, nothing is required from this account until around 2051.

11Section 8

Payment start date and frequency

Leave blank

Good news lives on this page: "you may still take a distribution by making separate withdrawal requests." She does not have to schedule recurring payments to get money out — she can request a withdrawal whenever she needs one, in whatever amount. That's exactly what the plan needs, since the yearly amounts differ.

12Section 9

Rollover instructions

Leave blank

Every field. The page's own margin says: "Do not complete this page if you chose Option 1 — stay in the plan as a beneficiary."

13Section 10

Where to send the money

Leave blank

Only for a cash withdrawal or a required payment. Since she's taking nothing now, there's nowhere to send anything. Bank details can be added later, online, when she does want a withdrawal.

14–15Section 11

Naming her own beneficiaries

Must fill in

Required with Option 1, and currently empty. This is who inherits the account after her — primary on page 14, backups on page 15. She can change it any time later, online.

Worth a moment's thought, not a reflex. If she names a child who is still a minor, a court may have to appoint a guardian before they could actually receive it. The usual fix is naming a custodian or a trust instead. One question for whoever is handling the estate.

16Section 12

Signature

Sign — with a notary

Sign, date, and add her Social Security number. Leave "Capacity" blank — that box is only for someone signing on another person's behalf.

Because the inheritance is over $200,000, this has to be signed in front of a notary — unless she already has her own TIAA account, which waives it. Banks and public libraries usually have one. The notary then completes page 17.

Don't sign until page 3 is resolved. That's what the signature certifies.

Before it goes in the envelope: page 3 blank or confirmed, the account list on page 1 checked, Section 11 filled in, and a notary lined up. Photograph or photocopy every page after signing — TIAA can lose things, and re-creating this is miserable.

This is a reading of the form, not legal or tax advice. It was written from photographs of the packet, and one page — the first, with the account list and balances — hasn't been reviewed. TIAA's own service line, 800-842-2252, can confirm anything here for free, and the two flagged items at the top are worth a professional's time before signing.
